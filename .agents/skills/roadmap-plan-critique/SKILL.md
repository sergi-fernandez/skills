# Critique a Roadmap, Launch Plan, or Product Plan

## Goal

Pressure-test a roadmap or plan so the PM can improve sequencing, scope, ownership, decision gates, and communication. The output should identify what is strong, what is weak, what should change, and what needs a decision.

## Inputs to request when missing

Ask only for context that changes the critique:

- Planning horizon and company objective.
- Target segment, product area, and current business priority.
- Team capacity, key constraints, dependencies, and non-negotiables.
- Current evidence for each major bet.
- Success metrics, guardrails, and review cadence.
- Launch, migration, or GTM constraints.

If unavailable, infer cautiously and list the assumptions that most affect sequencing.

## Senior PM standard

Critique the plan as an operating system for decisions, not a list of projects.

Look for:

- Whether the roadmap is a strategy translated into choices or a collection of stakeholder requests.
- Whether sequencing reduces uncertainty early or defers the riskiest unknowns.
- Whether capacity is protected for discovery, quality, launch, analytics, support, and unplanned work.
- Whether decision gates are strong enough to stop, narrow, or re-sequence work.
- Whether dependencies are real blockers, coordination tasks, or symptoms of unclear ownership.
- Whether the plan creates learning loops before irreversible investment.

## Plan lenses

Evaluate:

- Outcome clarity: whether the plan is tied to measurable customer or business outcomes.
- Sequencing: whether order reflects dependencies, learning, risk, and capacity.
- Scope control: whether non-goals, cuts, and tradeoffs are clear.
- Ownership: whether each workstream has a DRI and decision owner.
- Capacity: whether the plan is plausible for the team and time horizon.
- Dependencies: teams, vendors, data, legal, compliance, design, GTM, or platform work.
- Decision gates: points where evidence should change the plan.
- Rollout: launch strategy, migration, support, enablement, and communication.
- Measurement: success metrics, guardrails, baselines, and review cadence.

## Workflow

1. Restate the plan in neutral language.
2. Identify the intended outcome and time horizon.
3. Map workstreams, dependencies, owners, and sequencing.
4. Find failure modes:
   - Too many parallel bets.
   - Dependencies hidden until late.
   - Output-heavy plan with weak outcome logic.
   - No kill criteria.
   - No rollout or adoption path.
   - No capacity realism.
5. Identify the bets that should be pulled forward, delayed, narrowed, killed, or converted into discovery.
6. Recommend concrete changes to sequencing, scope, ownership, or validation.
7. Produce a stakeholder-ready version if requested.

## Output format

### Verdict

State whether the plan is ready, mostly ready with fixes, risky, or not yet decision-grade.

### What Works

List the strongest parts briefly.

### Critical Issues

List issues that could materially change success.

### Recommended Changes

Use a table:

| Change | Why it matters | Owner | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Bet-Level Recommendation

For major bets, state: continue, narrow, validate first, delay, combine, or kill.

### Decision Gates

List decisions, evidence needed, and review dates or milestones.

### Stakeholder Message

If useful, provide a concise message the PM can send.

## Quality bar

- Do not rewrite the plan generically.
- Be explicit about tradeoffs.
- Highlight decisions, not just tasks.
- Keep recommendations practical for the stated team and timeline.
- Make capacity and sequencing tradeoffs explicit.
